## The big picture

This module takes a model release apart. It answers three questions a download button hides: what you actually received, what you may do with it, and how far its reported numbers can be trusted. The word "open" settles none of them on its own.

It builds on *Post-training*, which separated a base model from the models adapted from it; *Scaling laws*, which related parameters, tokens and compute; and *How we know a model works*, which showed that one headline score can hide what matters.

### Where it shows up

- A model's release page or download, which lists or leaves out each part: weights, code, data information, license, evaluation results and cost figures.
- The **model card**, the structured document describing a release, and leaderboard scores from an **evaluation harness**, a scripted set of test items and scoring rules.
- At release, GPT-4 (2023) was API-only, Llama 2 (2023) shipped open weights under a custom license, Mistral 7B (2023) shipped weights under Apache-2.0 without its training data, and Pythia (2023) and OLMo (2024) released weights, training code and training data.
- The Open Source Initiative's Open Source AI Definition 1.0 (OSAID 1.0, October 2024), the definition of open source AI this module uses.

### Why it matters

Reading one part of a release as the whole, or a reported score as exact, leads to concrete mistakes:

- shipping a hosted product on weights licensed for research only;
- training another model on outputs that a license or an API's terms of service forbid;
- taking a permissive license as clearance for training data whose origin and consent are unknown;
- ranking two models on a 3-point gap from a 40-item harness, where a score of 70% has a **standard error** of about 7 points (the spread a score has from sampling its items alone).

### What you will be able to explain

- How a base model, a fine-tune, an API, open weights and open-source AI differ, and why permissively licensed weights alone fall short of open-source AI.
- How to check a proposed use against a license clause by clause, why the strictest clause decides, and why that permission says nothing about data provenance.
- How to read a model card's numbers critically: the standard error on a harness score, and what an estimated training-energy figure counts and leaves out.

### What the lab shows

Nothing is trained or run. harbor-lm-7b, its harness and its three licenses are fictional, with clause types drawn from real licenses. Every verdict is a lookup of those clauses, not legal advice. Two card fields are closed-form: the standard error for 40 items at 70%, and the training energy from 6 × parameters × tokens at a fixed A100 peak of 312 TFLOP/s, 40% utilization and 400 W per GPU.

- **Sample model card**: **License** sets the terms, and **Training code** and **Data information** set what ships beside the weights. A badge names the release tier, and a note lists what is still missing for OSAID 1.0.
- **Release spectrum**: no controls. Four columns run from API only to Open source AI, and the card's column is highlighted.
- **Is this use allowed**: **Intended use** picks one of four uses, and the card lists each clause that use needs as allowed, allowed with conditions, or not allowed. **License match** reports the strictest, beside **Clauses checked** and **Still unknown**.

The key experiment sets **License** to `Apache-2.0` and **Intended use** to `Hosted product`. **License match** says yes, but **Still unknown** keeps listing what the license cannot answer, because that readout depends only on **Data information**.

## What it is

A model release is a bundle of separable parts: weights, inference code, training code, data or
information about the data, a **license**, evaluation results, and cost figures. Each part can be
shipped, withheld, or restricted on its own.

A **base model** is next-token pretrained and is not yet an assistant. A **fine-tune** is a further,
narrower run on top of one. An **API** sells access to behavior without the weights. **Open weights**
means you can download the parameters, and nothing more is implied.

### Open weights are not open source

The Open Source Initiative published version 1.0 of its Open Source AI Definition in October 2024. It
asks for the parameters, the complete code used to train and run the system, and data information
detailed enough that a skilled person could build a substantially equivalent system. All of it must
come under terms that allow use for any purpose.

So **open source AI** differs from open weights on code, data information, and terms. A **permissive
license** such as Apache-2.0 on the weights alone still leaves a release short. The definition does
not require publishing all training data in every case.

### Permission is per use

A use needs specific permissions: commercial use, modification, redistribution, or using outputs to
train another model. The strictest clause it touches decides. An **acceptable-use policy** makes a
grant conditional, which is one reason custom model licenses do not meet the Open Source Definition.

A license only settles what its authors could grant. **Data provenance**, where the training data came
from and on what terms, is a separate question.

## Why it’s here

A deployment decision needs the legal answer, the data answer, and honest error bars on the
evaluation, and those live in different parts of the release.

Ethics and impact builds on these distinctions: it asks who pays the costs a card only hints at.

## How to play with it

1. Read **Sample model card** top to bottom before changing anything.
2. Set **License** to `Apache-2.0`, then **Training code** to `released` and **Data information** to
   `published`. Watch the highlighted column move in **Release spectrum**.
3. Set **Intended use** to `Hosted product`, then `Train on its outputs`, under `Research only` and
   `Custom community`, and read which clause blocks each.
4. Set **License** to `Apache-2.0` and **Intended use** to `Hosted product`. **License match** says
   yes; read **Still unknown**.

## What to notice

- `Research only` with training code and data information released is still restricted open weights.
  Terms matter as much as files.
- Under `Apache-2.0`, the hosted product is allowed and the data's sources and consent are still
  unknown.
- The card's 40-item harness has a standard error of about 7 points at 70%, so its 95% interval runs
  from about 56% to 84%. A leaderboard gap of 3 points on it means little.
- The energy row is computed: 6 × 7B × 2T ≈ 8.4×10²² FLOP, about 187,000 A100-hours at 40%
  utilization, about 75 MWh of GPU power. Meta reported 184,320 GPU-hours for Llama 2 7B, trained on
  the same 2T tokens.

## Where it breaks

**In this lab.** harbor-lm-7b, its harness, and all three licenses are fictional; the clause types are
drawn from real ones. The verdicts are a lookup of those clauses, not legal advice. The energy
estimate counts GPU board power for one successful run, with no cooling, CPUs, networking, failed runs,
or experiments.

**In real systems.** Licenses vary by version and jurisdiction, and custom model licenses change
between releases. API terms of service govern outputs even when you never see the weights. A fine-tune
usually inherits the base model's license and any problems in its data.

A card can also be wrong or silent. A missing field is information, and a harness score can be
inflated by test items leaking into training data.

## Going deeper

Examples at release, which later versions may change:

- GPT-4 (2023) was offered only through OpenAI's API and apps; its weights were not released.
- Llama 2 (2023) shipped open weights under a custom license with an acceptable-use policy and a
  separate-license requirement above 700 million monthly active users. OSI said it is not open source.
- Mistral 7B (2023) shipped weights under Apache-2.0, without its training data.
- EleutherAI's Pythia (2023) and AI2's OLMo (2024) released weights, training code, and training data.

**Ethics and impact** compares training energy with serving energy and asks who is represented in a
benchmark. **Evaluation** shows how a harness gets contaminated, and **Deployment & serving** is the
hosted-product path.
