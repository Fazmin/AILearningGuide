## The big picture

A **data mixture** splits a fixed **token budget** among **domains**: so many tokens of web text,
so many of code, so many of math. **Mid-training** is a late change to that split. Near the end of
pretraining, while the learning rate decays, a lab shifts the mixture toward the domains it most
wants the model to end on, and keeps some general text so the rest is not lost.

This module measures both halves. It builds on *Train one from scratch*, whose 900-weight character
bigram and minibatch SGD it reuses, on *Building a dataset*, where a mixture was one line in the
pipeline, and on *Synthetic data*, because one of its domains is generated from templates.

### Where it shows up

- **Pretraining mixtures.** Llama 3 reports a final pretraining mix of roughly 50% general
  knowledge, 25% math and reasoning, 17% code and 8% multilingual tokens (Grattafiori et al.,
  arXiv 2407.21783).
- **Mid-training.** OLMo 2 trains in two stages and calls the shorter second one, 5 to 10 percent
  of training compute, mid-training. It runs on Dolmino Mix 1124, filtered web, academic and
  encyclopedic text plus math, while the learning rate decays linearly to zero (Team OLMo, arXiv
  2501.00656).
- **Annealing.** In the last 40 million tokens of Llama 3's pretraining the rate anneals linearly
  to zero and the mix upsamples very high-quality sources.

### Why it matters

The budget is fixed before the run starts, so every share is a trade: more of one domain is less
of the others. The domains a lab most wants are also small, so upweighting them means repeating
them, and repetition loses value. And every batch moves the same weights, so *when* a domain is
seen changes where the weights settle, not only how often it was seen.

### What you will be able to explain

- How to read a data mixture as shares of a fixed token budget, and turn a weight into effective
  epochs.
- Why repeating scarce data helps at first, then stops helping, and with very little data starts to
  hurt.
- Why labs switch to a target-heavy mixture while the learning rate decays.
- How mid-training on a target mix differs from training on the target alone in what each costs the
  general domains.
- What per-domain held-out loss can and cannot tell you about a capability.

### What the lab shows

Every loss on the page is measured live, from runs that train the full 900-weight table from zeros
with plain SGD at rate 3, 16 pairs a batch, seed 7. Three domains are the course's fixed paragraphs:
Harbor (8 sentences), Recipes (8) and Proverbs (10). Arithmetic is synthetic, 160 facts such as
"four plus eight is twelve." generated from three templates with the numbers spelled out, because
the toy vocabulary has no digits. Each domain keeps the last quarter of its sentences, rounded to
whole sentences, out of training, and every loss is scored on those.

- **Data mixture** splits 600 steps, 9,600 character pairs, among the four domains with **Mixture
  preset** and four weight sliders.
- **Arithmetic weight sweep** reruns that mixture at every **Arithmetic weight** from 0 to 8.
- **Answer probe** asks the same trained table to complete 40 held-out facts.
- **Repeated data** trains on Arithmetic alone with a **Budget** and a number of **Unique
  sentences**.
- **Late phase and annealing** switches the mixture at step 480, where the
  **warmup-stable-decay** schedule starts to decay. Its schedule curve is the closed-form formula
  the trainer applies, not a measurement.

The key experiment: set **Late phase** to **Target mix**, and the run that ends on Arithmetic beats
one that sees the same Arithmetic spread evenly, at a cost to General.

## What it is

### Shares of one budget

A **mixture weight** is one number per domain, and only its ratio to the others counts. The lab
divides each weight by the sum of the four to get a share. Each of the 16 slots in a batch then
picks a domain with probability equal to its share and takes that domain's next pair from its own
shuffled order.

The default, **General-heavy**, is weights 3, 3, 3 and 1: 30% each for Harbor, Recipes and
Proverbs and 10% for Arithmetic. **Uniform** is 1, 1, 1 and 1. Over 600 steps of 16 pairs the
**token budget** is 9,600 character pairs, and the shares split that fixed total.

### From shares to epochs

**Effective epochs** are the pairs drawn from a domain divided by the training pairs it holds.
Domains differ in size: Harbor's six training sentences hold 378 pairs, Recipes' six hold 292,
Proverbs' seven hold 195, and Arithmetic's 120 hold 3,417.

So equal weights do not mean equal epochs. Uniform gives Proverbs 12.48 epochs and Arithmetic 0.70.
General-heavy gives Harbor 7.42, Recipes 10.21, Proverbs 14.72 and Arithmetic 0.28.

Giving a domain a larger share of the budget than its share of the text is **upsampling**.
Arithmetic is 80% of the training text, so General-heavy upsamples the three small general domains
and downsamples Arithmetic to one batch slot in ten.

### Scoring each domain

**Per-domain held-out loss** is cross-entropy, in nats per character, on one domain's held-out
sentences: 2 of Harbor's 8, 2 of Recipes' 8, 3 of Proverbs' 10 and 40 of the 160 arithmetic facts.
**General** is the mean of the three general domains' held-out losses, each counted once. A table
that knows nothing scores ln 30 = 3.401.

### The schedule and the late phase

**Warmup-stable-decay** ramps the rate up over the first 5% of steps, holds 3 through the stable
phase, and decays it linearly to zero over the last 20%, from step 480. **Annealing** is that final
decay. **Late phase** can change the mixture exactly there:

- **Same mix** keeps the weights to the end.
- **Target mix** makes half of every late batch Arithmetic and splits the other half among the
  general domains in their own proportions. This is the shape of **mid-training**.
- **Target only** makes every late batch Arithmetic, close to training on the target alone.

A late switch is the simplest **curriculum**: one mixture for most of the run, another at the end.
**Forgetting** here is General held-out at the end minus General held-out at step 480, measured on
the same run.

## Why it’s here

### Data is scarce where it matters most

The domains a lab most wants, such as math, code and carefully filtered text, are a small part of
what can be collected, and a run's budget is far larger than any one of them. A mixture that
upweights them has to repeat them. That is **data-constrained training**.

Muennighoff et al. trained models under exactly that constraint. Up to 4 epochs of repeated data
changed loss very little compared with unique data, while with much more repetition the value of
extra compute decayed to zero (arXiv 2305.16264). The Repeated data card asks a toy the same
question.

### Why the end of the run matters

Every batch moves the same weights, so the order of the data changes the result, not only its
totals. The last steps have the final say, and the decaying rate sets how far they can move the
weights.

Labs use this. The MiniCPM authors, who introduced the WSD schedule, mixed high-quality data into
the decay and found it worked better than adding the same data only at fine-tuning (Hu et al., arXiv
2404.06395). Llama 3 found that annealing on small amounts of high-quality code and math raised its
8B model's GSM8k score by 24.0% and MATH by 6.4%, while gains for the 405B model were negligible.
OLMo 2's GSM8K rose from 24.1 to 67.5 for the 7B model from the end of pretraining to the end of
mid-training.

WSD makes this cheap. Its stable phase does not depend on the run's length, and a constant rate
with a cooldown scales as reliably as cosine decay (Hägele et al., arXiv 2405.18392). So a lab can
branch one checkpoint into several decays with different mixtures.

### Mid-training versus fine-tuning

A late phase that drops the general data entirely is continued training on the target alone, close
to fine-tuning. Nothing in its loss mentions the general domains, so the shared weights drift toward
the target, which is **forgetting**. Mid-training keeps general text in the late mix to prevent
that.

Ibrahim et al. found that new data degrades performance on earlier data, and that replaying earlier
data while re-warming and re-decaying the rate matches retraining from scratch (arXiv 2403.08763).

## How to play with it

### Step 1: set the mixture

Under **Mixture preset** press **Uniform**: each domain gets a quarter of the batches. The Epochs
column reads 6.16 for Harbor, 8.34 for Recipes, 12.48 for Proverbs and 0.70 for Arithmetic. Held-out
loss reads 2.197, 2.498, 2.400 and 1.967, and General 2.365.

Now press **General-heavy**. Harbor rises to 7.42 epochs and falls to 2.097, and Recipes to 10.21
epochs and 2.427. Proverbs rises to 14.72 epochs and moves from 2.400 to 2.408: past 12 passes,
more passes over seven short proverbs buy nothing. Arithmetic pays, from 1.967 to 2.348, and General
improves from 2.365 to 2.311.

### Step 2: upweight the scarce domain

Arithmetic is scarce in this mix, not in text. Keep the general weights at 3, 3, 3 and drag
**Arithmetic weight** from 0 to 8. On **Arithmetic weight sweep**, Arithmetic held-out falls from
2.832 to 1.706 while General held-out rises from 2.320 to 2.515.

**Gain per extra epoch** shrinks down the column: 1.75 nats per epoch from weight 0 to 1, 1.04 from 1
to 2, 0.25 from 7 to 8. Even weight 8 buys only 1.32 epochs of Arithmetic, so this shrinking gain is
the learning curve flattening, not repetition. Weight 0 still scores 2.832, below 3.401, because
letter habits learned from the general domains carry over.

### Step 3: repeat or find more

On **Repeated data**, keep **Budget** at 8,000 pairs and lower **Unique sentences** from 120. The
bars hold the budget fixed and halve the data each time:

- 120 sentences: 2.3 epochs, held-out 1.388.
- 60: 4.7 epochs, 1.400.
- 30: 9.4 epochs, 1.458.
- 15: 18.8 epochs, 1.577.
- 8: 34.6 epochs, 1.731.
- 4: 70.8 epochs, 1.961.
- 2: 135.6 epochs, 2.542.

At 2 unique sentences, **Lowest along the run** reads 2.217 at 27.1 epochs, and the dashed line
climbs for the rest of the budget.

### Step 4: anneal on the target mix

Set **Late phase** to **Target mix**. From step 480, half of every batch is Arithmetic. The table
compares three runs:

- **Switched at decay**: Arithmetic 1.911, General 2.398, Forgetting +0.060, 1,721 Arithmetic pairs.
- **Spread evenly**: 2.110, 2.339, −0.027, 1,707 pairs.
- **No switch**: 2.348, 2.311, −0.027, 943 pairs.

Spread evenly draws the same pairs per domain on average, in one constant mixture, so the gap between
the first two columns is timing alone. Arithmetic falls from 2.356 at step 480 to 1.911 in the last
120 steps.

### Step 5: mid-train or fine-tune?

Switch **Late phase** to **Target only**. Switched at decay now reads Arithmetic 1.645, General
2.691 and Forgetting +0.353, against +0.060 with Target mix. Spread evenly reads 1.935 and 2.375.
Target only buys 0.266 more nats on Arithmetic and costs 0.293 on General.

## What to notice

- The budget is fixed, so every bar that falls on **Data mixture** is paid for by one that rises.
- Equal weights give unequal epochs, and the smallest domain runs out of new text first.
- Upweighting a domain buys less with each step: Gain per extra epoch falls from 1.75 to 0.25.
- Repetition is nearly free at first and costly later. At 8,000 pairs, **Versus 120 unique** reads
  +0.011 for 60 sentences and +0.069 for 30. It subtracts the unrounded losses, so it can differ by
  0.001 from the rounded bars.
- With very little data, more passes make held-out loss worse: 2 sentences bottom out at 2.217 and
  end at 2.542.
- Timing matters even when the data does not change. Switched and Spread evenly draw 1,721 and
  1,707 Arithmetic pairs and end 0.199 nats apart on Arithmetic.
- Forgetting tracks how much general text the late phase keeps: −0.027 with no switch, +0.060 with
  Target mix, +0.353 with Target only.

## Where it breaks

### In this lab

- **Toy domains.** Three domains are fixed paragraphs of 8 to 10 sentences, and Arithmetic is
  generated from three templates. Their sizes, not their real value, decide where repetition sets
  in.
- **A bigram model.** The table predicts each character from the one before it, so domains differ
  only in which letter tends to follow which.
- **One seed.** At the default mixture, seeds 1 to 8 move Arithmetic held-out by up to 0.07 nats and
  General held-out by about 0.01.
- **Fixed choices.** General is a plain mean of three losses. The switch is always at step 480, the
  Target mix share is always 50%, and the warmup is always 5%.
- **Closed-form parts.** The learning-rate curve and the Arithmetic share curve on **Late phase and
  annealing** are the formulas the trainer applies, drawn from the settings.

### What the loss misses

On **Answer probe** the default table gets 0 of 40 held-out facts right. It gives the same answer,
ten, to every question, and ten is never the answer to a held-out fact here. A bigram sees only the
space before the answer, so it cannot read the numbers in the question.

Set **Arithmetic weight** to 0 and Arithmetic held-out rises to 2.832, yet the probe gets 3 of 40
right, because it now answers one and three held-out answers happen to be one. The loss fell by
learning how number words are spelled, not how to add. **Per-domain held-out loss** scores prediction
of a domain's text, and a capability needs its own test.

### Where the toy disagrees

- **The decay buys retention, not a bigger gain.** With Target mix at **Constant rate**, Arithmetic
  ends at 1.785, lower than 1.911 with the decay, and General at 2.462, worse than 2.398. Forgetting
  doubles, from +0.060 to +0.127. In the toy most of the late-data gain comes from being last, and
  full-size late steps pull the weights furthest toward it. Published annealing results attribute
  their gains to target data during the decay; this toy reproduces the switch's advantage over an
  even spread but not a boost from the decay itself.
- **The decay barely helps on its own.** With Same mix, General falls by 0.027 over the last 20% of
  steps with the decay and by 0.022 at a constant rate. MiniCPM's authors report a steep loss drop
  once their decay starts.
- **Repetition costs variety here, not memory.** A bigram cannot memorize a sentence, so repeating
  data hurts it by narrowing which letter pairs it sees. Large models memorize: Hernandez et al.
  found that repeating 0.1% of the data 100 times can degrade an 800-million-parameter model to the
  level of one half its size (arXiv 2205.10487).

### In real systems

A lower loss on math text is not a better mathematician, which is why OLMo 2 reports GSM8K, a
benchmark of held-out problems, after mid-training. Real mixtures cover tens of sources at trillions
of tokens, and their weights come from runs of small proxy models. A weight that wins at small scale
need not win at full scale. Forgetting is measured on suites of held-out tasks, not on one averaged
loss.

## Going deeper

### Choosing weights without guessing

DoReMi trains a small proxy model with group distributionally robust optimization over the domains
and reads the domain weights from it. On The Pile it improved perplexity across all domains, even
those it downweighted, raised average few-shot accuracy by 6.5 points, and reached the baseline's
accuracy in 2.6 times fewer steps (Xie et al., arXiv 2305.10429).

Data mixing laws fit a function from mixture proportions to loss on small runs, then predict
unseen mixtures before training them (Ye et al., arXiv 2403.16952). It is the **Arithmetic weight
sweep** turned into a formula you can optimize.

### Annealing as a cheap data test

Llama 3 judged candidate datasets by annealing a half-trained 8B model to zero over 40 billion
tokens, with 30% of the weight on the candidate and 70% on the default mix. Blakeney et al.
upsampled domain data at the end of training a 7B model on 1 trillion tokens. They gained up to
6.90 points on MMLU, 8.26 on GSM8K and 6.17 on HumanEval, and found 10 to 20 percent of training
the best trade-off with general ability (arXiv 2406.03476).

### Beyond one switch

SmolLM2 trains in several stages that mix web text with math, code and instruction data, and
refines each stage's mixing rates by hand from the previous stage's results (Ben Allal et al.,
arXiv 2502.02737). OLMo 2 runs mid-training several times with different data orders and averages
the resulting weights. Llama 3 averages checkpoints from its final anneal.

### Continued pretraining and fine-tuning

Gururangan et al. showed that a second phase of pretraining on in-domain text improves tasks in that
domain (arXiv 2004.10964). **Target only** is that idea at the end of a run. The next lab,
*Fine-tuning & transfer*, starts from a trained model and measures forgetting under fine-tuning;
*Hyperparameters & schedules* covers the schedule; *Evaluation* covers the benchmarks a mixture is
finally judged on.

### Toy versus real

- **Budget.** This lab spends 9,600 character pairs; OLMo 2's 7B model trained on about 4 trillion
  tokens.
- **Decay length.** The late phase here is 20% of steps. MiniCPM found a decay over about 10% of
  tokens enough, and OLMo 2's mid-training is 5 to 10 percent of compute.
- **Mixture.** General-heavy is 30, 30, 30 and 10 percent of four tiny domains; Llama 3's final mix
  is roughly 50, 25, 17 and 8 percent of trillions of tokens.
- **Evaluation.** This lab reads per-domain loss and one probe; real mid-training is judged on
  benchmarks of held-out problems.
