## The big picture

This module asks how a trained network can make a whole new image from a compact code or from noise.
A **variational autoencoder (VAE)** compresses each image to a short code, its **latent**, and
decodes a code to an image in one forward pass. A **diffusion** model trains a **denoiser** to
predict the noise added to an image, then generates by removing noise from a fresh draw over many
sequential steps.

The encoder and decoder are stacked dense layers like those built in Stacking neurons, and the
denoiser is a convolutional U-Net, made of hidden layers that share their weights across image
positions. That module's contrast between memorizing and learning the pattern returns here: an exact
denoiser that copies, beside a trained one that draws new digits.

### Where it shows up

- **Image, audio and video generation.** Diffusion is a widely used mechanism behind all three.
- **Latent diffusion.** Many image generators, Stable Diffusion among them, run diffusion inside an
  autoencoder's compressed latent and then decode the result once.
- **Prompted generation.** The prompt enters the denoiser through cross-attention, and the scale of
  **classifier-free guidance** sets how strongly the sample follows it.
- **Newer formulations.** Flow matching, which trains the network to predict a velocity carrying
  noise to data, underlies several recent image models. Distillation cuts a sampler's many steps to
  a few.
- **Text.** Diffusion language models update every position of a passage together rather than one
  token at a time, but have trailed autoregressive models in quality.

### Why it matters

Both families generate from a random draw, a latent code or a noise image, rather than by looking an
image up, and they pay different costs. A VAE decodes in one pass but blurs, because a pixel-wise
loss scores a blend of plausible digits above any single guess. Diffusion makes one network call per
step, each waiting for the last. Three pieces each prevent a specific failure:

- **A schedule that ends in noise.** Generation starts from a fresh noise draw, so the forward
  process has to end at almost pure noise. Otherwise the denoiser starts from inputs unlike its
  training data, and the samples stay noisy.
- **Reparameterization.** Backpropagation cannot pass through a random draw, so the reconstruction
  loss would never reach the encoder. Drawing the noise as a separate input lets it through.
- **A network short of the optimum.** Sampling with the minimum-error denoiser for a finite training
  set can only return one of its images, so new samples need a network that does not reach that
  optimum.

### What you will be able to explain

- Contrast iterative denoising (one denoiser call per step, each needing the previous output) with
  latent reconstruction (one decoder pass from a compact code).
- Interpret a continuous latent space: why nearby codes decode to similar digits, and why the
  decoder blurs where digit regions meet.

### What the lab shows

The VAE's encoder and decoder and the diffusion denoiser were trained on MNIST handwritten digits,
ship as ONNX model files and run in the app; each card's status chip says what is running. **Model**
switches between `Diffusion` and `VAE`, and the diffusion half noises the VAE decoder's output at
the current latent point.

**Forward process** noises that digit in closed form, with no network; only its
`Noise-prediction error by step` chart is measured, on this one digit and noise draw. In
**Reverse diffusion**, `Denoiser` picks the `Trained network` or the `Ideal denoiser`, exact
arithmetic over 64 VAE-drawn references rather than a model. `Start from` and `Noise seed` set the
starting point and the randomness, and **Denoiser calls** scrubs the run from noise to sample.

**Latent map** shows the real decoder on a 16 × 16 grid of latent points: drag the point, or use
`Latent z₁` and `Latent z₂`. **Encode a drawing** sends a drawing or a hand-drawn preset through the
real encoder and decoder. For that one image it prints the reconstruction's binary cross-entropy
(BCE) and the KL divergence (KL) of the encoder's Gaussian from the prior.

Dataset-wide figures in the lesson and card notes, such as classifier readings of generated samples,
are fixed offline measurements. The key experiment keeps one `Noise seed` and switches `Denoiser`:
the ideal denoiser lands exactly on one reference, and the trained network draws a digit that is
none of them.

## What it is

Every other lab in this course predicts the next token. This one covers the other main family of
generative models: those that produce a whole image at once, either from a compact code or from
noise. Both halves of the lab run real models trained on MNIST digits.

### The VAE: compress, then decode

A **variational autoencoder** trains two networks together. The **encoder** maps an image to a
distribution over a low-dimensional **latent** space: a mean μ and a log-variance for each dimension.
The **decoder** maps a latent point back to an image in one forward pass.

The shipped VAE has a two-dimensional latent, so every image becomes a point you can see on a map.
Its training loss, per image, is:

- a reconstruction term, binary cross-entropy summed over 784 pixels;
- plus 0.35 × a **KL divergence** that pulls each encoder Gaussian toward the standard normal prior.

Sampling is not differentiable, so training uses the **reparameterization** `z = μ + σ ⊙ ε` with ε
drawn separately. The randomness becomes an input and gradients flow through μ and σ.

### Diffusion: destroy, then learn to undo

The forward process is fixed and needs no learning. With a **noise schedule** of variances `β_t`,
the noised image at any step has a closed form:

`q(x_t | x₀) = N(√ᾱ_t · x₀, (1 − ᾱ_t) I)`, where `ᾱ_t` is the running product of `1 − β_t`.

The schedule has to end at almost pure noise, because generation starts from a fresh noise draw.
This lab's cosine schedule leaves √ᾱ = 0.031 of the digit at the last step.

The **denoiser** is trained for one job: given `x_t` and `t`, predict the noise ε that was added.
Generation, the **reverse process**, starts from noise and applies the DDPM update once per step:

`x_(t−1) = (x_t − β_t / √(1 − ᾱ_t) · ε̂) / √α_t + √β_t · z`

Each step is one network call, and each call needs the previous one's output. The shipped denoiser
is a small convolutional U-Net with 1,181,217 parameters, trained for 45 epochs on all 60,000
MNIST training digits.

## Why it’s here

Diffusion and VAEs are a useful contrast: not every generative model works autoregressively. This
lab's VAE also turns the latent space into something you can navigate rather than a hidden
implementation detail.

The two approaches are complementary, a point `Going deeper` returns to.

## What to notice

- Adding noise is one line of arithmetic. Only the reverse direction needs a trained network.
- With seed 7 from `Pure noise`, the trained network turns static into a digit: **Background pixels**
  climb from 0.17 to 0.82. The ideal denoiser, given the same noise, lands exactly on reference #64.
  The network's sample is nearest to reference #28, at a squared distance of 0.26, and is none of them.
- The ideal denoiser holds its weight spread over many references at first. At seed 7 one reference
  has half the weight after 15 calls and 99% after 32, and from then on x̂₀ is that reference.
- The network's noise-prediction error is 0.60 at `t = 0`, its worst, and under 0.001 at `t = 99`.
  Predicting ε is hardest when there is little of it.
- On the latent map, nearby points decode to similar digits. Each digit owns a region, the regions
  meet in a crowded middle, and the decoder blurs there.
- The `7` preset encodes to μ = (1.55, 0.02), among the 3s, and decodes as a 3. Its BCE is 211.6
  nats and its KL 6.26. The `3` preset comes back as a blurry 3 (BCE 121.1). The 7 shows a
  different failure: a wrong digit, not only a blurry one.

## Where it breaks

### In this lab

- **The denoiser is small and unconditional.** It has 1,181,217 parameters, saw only 28 × 28 MNIST
  digits, and takes no label, so the seed picks the digit and you cannot ask for one. Offline, an
  independent classifier read 256 of its pure-noise samples with a mean confidence of 0.95, over
  0.9 for 86% of them, against 0.99 for real test digits. The rest are malformed or ambiguous.
- **The samples are new, not copies.** Their average distance to the nearest training digit is 4.45,
  against 4.11 for real test digits, and 2% of them sit within 1.5 of one, against 4% of real test
  digits.
- **The ideal denoiser is arithmetic, not a model.** It is the exact posterior mean over 64
  reference digits drawn by the VAE decoder, and it can only ever return one of them. From pure
  noise, seeds 1 to 24 end on 20 different references.
- **The schedule is not exactly pure noise.** √ᾱ is 0.031 at the last step, so `Noised x₀` and
  `Pure noise` give almost the same sample. The sampler is plain 100-step DDPM, with no guidance.
- **The VAE is two-dimensional on purpose.** Its codes spread wider than the prior (standard
  deviations 1.41 and 1.55), and 15% of test digits land beyond radius 3, where the prior puts
  1.1%. A draw from the prior mostly hits the crowded middle: 48% of 10,000 prior draws are read
  with confidence above 0.9, against 86% of the network's samples.
- Presets and drawings are not MNIST digits; they skip MNIST's size normalization and centring.

### In real systems

VAE samples are characteristically blurry: a pixel-wise reconstruction loss rewards hedging, so
averaging several plausible outputs scores better than committing to one. With a KL weight below 1,
as here, the objective is a β-VAE loss rather than the exact negative ELBO.

Diffusion sampling needs many sequential network calls. Guidance at high strength causes
oversaturation. Diffusion models can reproduce individual training images, especially duplicated
ones (Carlini et al., 2023).

Likelihoods are awkward for both families. A VAE gives a lower bound (the ELBO), and a diffusion
model gives a bound or an expensive estimate, unlike the exact per-token likelihood of an
autoregressive model. Common image metrics compare feature distributions and miss failures a human
notices at once.

## Going deeper

### Why a real network can generate new images

The ideal denoiser shows that the exact optimum of the training objective, on a finite set,
memorizes. Trained networks generalize because they do not reach it: their architecture and limited
capacity smooth between training images. That gap between optimum and practice is where new samples
come from, and the lab shows it: same noise, same seed, one copy and one new digit.

### Combining the two ideas

**Latent diffusion** trains an autoencoder that shrinks each image side by a factor of 8, runs
diffusion in that spatial latent, and decodes once at the end. The cost reduction made
high-resolution generation practical.

Text prompts enter through cross-attention, so the denoiser predicts noise *given* a description.
**Classifier-free guidance** extrapolates from the unconditional prediction toward the conditional
one. Guidance scale is the most consequential setting: too low ignores the prompt, too high
oversaturates.

### Newer formulations

**Flow matching** and rectified flow train a network to predict a velocity field that carries noise
to data along nearly straight paths. Several recent image models, such as Stable Diffusion 3 and
Flux, use it. Separately, distillation compresses a many-step sampler into 1 to 4 steps.

Diffusion has also been applied to text, refining all positions in parallel instead of one token at
a time as in the inference lab. Quality has trailed autoregressive models, and it remains active
work.
